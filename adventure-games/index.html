Dragonkeep

Draw a dungeon floor plan and walk through it. Volume, surface area, and a dragon you win by not killing.

▶ Play Dragonkeep

What students practice

  • Measuring a floor plan as they draw it: area, perimeter, volume, wall area and surface area update with every cell.
  • Same volume, different wall: two rooms with the same area can have very different perimeters.
  • Surface area is not additive: push two rooms together and the shared wall stops being surface.
  • The square-cube law: double every dimension and the volume grows eight times while the wall grows four.

The thinking it makes visible

A floor plan raised to a ceiling height is a prism. Students draw the prism, the game measures it, and then they walk through it, so every number is something they can see and step through.

How to play

The two modes

Creator. An 11×11 grid — the original's maze footprint. Carve floor out of solid rock, place arms, treasure, monsters and puzzles, set the ceiling height, mist, wake radius and party size. Six measurements update live:

Perimeter Pcell edges facing rock or the outside
Volume VA · h
Wall areaP · h

Maze indexP ÷ 4√A

The perimeter is drawn thick on the plan, so the number in the panel is also a thing you can see.

Play. Corridors render as nested rectangles with flat sprites centred in them — the way the original did it, not raycast. Two parallel health tracks, War and Spiritual, with two matching weapon classes: swords and bows hit War, books and fireballs hit Spiritual.

While you play, one thin strip across the top carries what you check on every step — bricks, what the seal needs, how far the dragon is — and one line at the bottom says what just happened. The floor turns red within the dragon's wake radius, counted in steps along the corridors; step onto it, or strike the dragon, and it wakes, burns you and drives you back to where you came in. Everything else — health, helpers, treasure, the full map, the quest, and what the dungeon still holds — is in the status panel (I, Start, or the button on the strip), and the game waits while it is open.

Masonry is a wall someone built rather than rock: it shows as brick, and B lifts a brick out of it as readily as it lays one. Every brick you lay is masonry, so any brick can be taken back. The Montresor Vaults, after Poe's "The Cask of Amontillado", turns the dragon's puzzle around: Fortunato is walled up somewhere in the vaults, his bells grow louder as you near him, and the strip shows LIFT — the fewest bricks between you and him, counted exactly as the seal is. Lift the fewest there are and the game notices.

Dungeons also hold scrolls, which tell the story of the place and stay in the status panel once read; riddles on the way out, answered in one word; and things that want something — a windlass with no handle, a statue with an empty hand — that open once you bring what they need. A world's guide greets you in each dungeon and travels as your first helper. Riddles: Easy lets you through after three wrong answers and shows you the answer; Answer never does. Wrong answers cost nothing in Adventure.

The first world written this way is Wieliczka, the salt mine near Kraków, with Bartek, a salt miner, as guide.

Under the game sits a strip of cards: the finished worlds and dungeons, ready to play, with Battle / Adventure at its head. In Adventure nothing fights back — walk into a creature to collect it. Playing a card never touches your own drawing; it is waiting in Creator when you go back.

You win by not killing the dragon

The dragon cannot be fought. Strike it and it wakes. Come within k open steps — path distance through floor, not straight line — and it wakes. Either way you die.

You win by walling it in with the bricks your party carries, then walking out while it still breathes. Which makes "is this level winnable?" a minimum vertex cut: the fewest cells you must turn to rock to separate the entrance from the dragon, counting only cells outside the wake radius. The Creator panel solves it exactly with node-split max-flow and reports the number.

Bricks come from the party: 2 base plus 2 per helper. Helpers also step in front of one monster blow each, and then they are gone.

Because bricking only ever removes floor, path distances can only grow — so laying a brick can push a previously fatal cell out of the wake radius. You build your way to safety.

Lantern Run

An expedition in the Amazon. Twenty-four species, by night and by day. Thirty minutes. A retro jungle game for an NES-style controller.

▶ Play Lantern Run

In it now

  • A field journal records one true fact about each of the 24 species you document, across the four layers of the rainforest (floor, understory, canopy, emergent), by night and by day.

Ideas we are considering

  • Richer facts about each animal.
  • A sketch of each animal in the journal.
  • Hints about which layer of the forest an animal lives in.
How to play

You're a zoologist. The rainforest has four layers: the forest floor, the understory, the canopy and the emergent treetops, and each species lives in its own. Twelve species come out by night and twelve by day. Find all twenty-four before your time runs out.

To document a species, hold B and stand still with the animal in view until the circle fills.

  • By night you see only what your lantern reaches. Holding B raises it so you see further. Animals' eyes shine in the dark before you can see the rest of them.
  • By day you can see everything, and so can they. Holding B brings up binoculars, so you can observe from much further away (face the animal). Run near a shy animal and it bolts: birds fly off, monkeys scatter, an iguana drops off its branch. Creep (hold B while moving) to get closer.

Switching night and day: open the field journal with Select and press B. Dusk or dawn falls over two seconds, and the night animals slip away as the day ones come out, or the other way round. Nocturnal species only appear at night, diurnal ones only by day. Jaguars, anacondas and caimans are about at both times.

A standard USB gamepad works too, including cheap pads that report the D-pad as an analog stick.

The jungle is a loop: keep going one way and you come back to where you started. Creeks cross the forest floor. Some have a vine to swing across; caimans live in others, and you can hop across their heads while their jaws are shut. At night, lightning lights the whole forest now and then. Look while you can.

Watch out for: jaguars, anacondas and the fer-de-lance (it can sense your warmth in total darkness). A vampire bat bite costs you time, and so does touching a poison dart frog: never touch one. A startled armadillo jumps straight up.

Browsers start sound only after a key press or click (a gamepad button may not count), so press any key once if it is silent.

Points: 500 for a species on the forest floor, more the higher it lives. A time bonus if you find all twenty-four.
