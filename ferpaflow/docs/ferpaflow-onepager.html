FerpaFlow

A FERPA-aware pipeline for letting AI help with grading — without putting grades or identifiable student records into a system that isn't cleared to hold them.

Draft · May 2026 · for colleagues in Teacher Education & Computer Science · not legal advice, not yet reviewed by counsel · MIT-licensed · OhioMathTeacher / TEA

Faculty want AI help with feedback and grading. Today the realistic options are (a) paste student work into consumer ChatGPT / Gemini / Claude and hope — no institutional agreement, no idea where the data goes — or (b) don't use AI at all. FerpaFlow is a third path. It's mostly a question of where the data lives, not a loophole.

The idea — split the workflow into three zones

Zone 1 · Submissions

An institution-controlled git host

A campus GitLab / Gitea, or a department server. (A private GitHub repo only as a one-person pilot stopgap.) Students submit classwork here.

Crosses this boundary: student classwork  ·  no grades

│  collector.py polls outward — no open ports  │
▼
Zone 2 · Feedback

The institution's sanctioned cloud model

At Miami: Google Gemini / NotebookLM, under the Workspace agreement we already have. Returns rubric-aligned comments — strengths, gaps, suggestions tied to criteria.

Crosses this boundary: de-identified work + rubric — scrubbed and human-reviewed first  ·  no scores, ever

│  comments returned to the student via the git host  │
▼  (separately) ▼
Zone 3 · Grades

A local LLM on the faculty member's own computer

Ollama, no internet connection. Produces a grade recommendation + rationale, shown on the faculty member's screen and saved to a local log. The faculty member reads it and types the grade into Canvas by hand.

Stays here: the grade  ·  local only — never leaves the machine

The move that makes it work: the step that produces a grade runs only on faculty hardware; the step that touches the cloud produces only comments, on de-identified text, through an already-vetted tool. Risk is concentrated where it can be contained — and contained there.

What's honest about the legal side

We do not claim "student classwork isn't a FERPA record." An earlier draft leaned on Owasso v. Falvo (2002) for that — we've dropped it. Falvo only says peer-graded papers being passed among students before the teacher records the grade aren't "education records," not that classwork is categorically unprotected. FerpaFlow relies instead on data minimization (grades & identifiers stay local) and sanctioned endpoints (only tools the institution has vetted) — which hold regardless. Open questions for counsel: is "scrubbed work → comments" inside our Google agreement; what counts as adequate de-identification of student writing; must students be told feedback is AI-assisted.

Why it matters for our department

  • A model for preservice teachers. "Design a compliant AI workflow" beats "don't get caught." A FERPA-literacy layer (the Owasso facts, a "what gets you fired" scenario guide, a build-your-own-pipeline worksheet) drops into the TCE / CSE AI-bridge coursework.
  • A bridge between tools Miami already has. Google Workspace, Canvas, and faculty interest all exist; the missing piece is a principled workflow connecting them. This is a working sketch of that seam — and it shows local AI and the institutional Google tools are complementary, not competing (relevant to the NVIDIA-grant exploration).
  • It scales without redesign. Same architecture, one box swapped — see below.

Scales to campus without a redesign

Single-faculty today → campus tomorrow is the same architecture with one box swapped: Zone 1 becomes a campus-run GitLab / Gitea behind SSO; Zone 3 becomes an IT-managed local-inference appliance (the kind of thing the NVIDIA-grant exploration points at); Zone 2 is unchanged — the Google Workspace tooling we already license. The faculty workstation just becomes a managed campus service.

Status — May 2026

Working: the local grader (grader.py → Ollama, a sample YAML rubric, a local audit log) and a heuristic PII tripwire. Documented, not yet built: the git-host collector and the cloud-feedback step — deliberately; the Google-agreement questions come first. Repo to be created; the scaffold is ready to drop in.

The ask

Not "adopt this." A small pilot + a legal review. One assignment, one section, one faculty machine — then take what we learn to IT and counsel.
