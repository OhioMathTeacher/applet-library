🧩 Applet Library

A collection of interactive web applets for mathematics and science education.

Talks

  • 💡 Who Gets to See the Light
    An hour for teachers on free AI in the classroom, kept in one place for anyone who was in the room or wishes they had been. The recording, the slides, Two math games, six questions (a one-page tool for judging a math game, with a blank column for yours), and the two openers — Reach and Name Game. Includes the three apps of the talk — Allegory, Clique and Marginalia — with a three-step recipe for running a free model on your own laptop, wifi off.

Teaching & Learning

  • 🏛️ Marginalia
    A reading-and-writing studio: read and annotate five source documents — Plato's Allegory of the Cave, Gandhi, Confucius, Bryant and Ibsen — with the interface in six languages. Select any passage to think in the margin: freewrite privately in the spirit of Peter Elbow, send passages to a dated Writer's Journal, and call on two AI thinking partners, Socrates who questions and Elbow who encourages. Everything saves to your browser; export to JSON for backup or transfer.
  • ☎️ Telefone
    Telephone, except the machine is every player. Hand it a page, ask it to revise, and watch what it corrects away — each round revises the last one, so the losses compound, and each tab wears the share of the student's words still standing. It ends itself when under a fifth survive, leaving round 0 beside the ending round, which is the finding. Bring your own model; nothing is submitted and nothing is graded.
  • 🗂️ Sort Lab
    An open-sort surface for talking about how learners categorize. Students drag items into groups they invent themselves, then name the rule behind each group. Six sets ship with it, and any item can be turned or flipped, so a class can test whether orientation belongs in the rule; the interface runs in English, Spanish and Chinese.
  • 🧠 Watch Your Own Thinking
    Three metacognitive math puzzles that record how you solve, then hand the reasoning back to you: Metadoku (constraint deduction), Metaplete (hypothesis-testing) and Metagons (inverse reasoning). Infer the rules from a worked example, then solve with every move quietly time-stamped, scrub back through your own solve and attach notes at the moments that carried real reasoning. Then generate an editable “thinking analysis” — your strategy, in your words. No data leaves the device.
  • 🪢 Clique
    A Connections-style word puzzle game for the classroom: build, share and play 4×4 puzzles where players group 16 items into four hidden categories. Puzzles are organized by grade band and content area.
  • 🍬 Integer Partitioning
    Explore integer partitions through jelly bean distribution, Ferrers diagrams, and constraint filters.
  • 🌳 Tree Diagram Builder
    Build probability tree diagrams interactively, with live validation that sibling probabilities sum to 1 and automatic computation of joint probabilities at each leaf. Sandbox mode lets students add and edit branches; example problems load from JSON files (bundled or teacher-authored) at three scaffolding tiers — fully worked, partially completed, and blank.
  • 🪐 Orbit Explorer
    Explore the dynamics of Collatz-style functions: set the parameters m, b and d to define a function pair, seed the iteration with a starting value, watch the trajectory and any cycle it falls into, and save interesting cases for comparison. Includes an AI thinking partner — pick any model in the 🤖 AI Setup menu.

Utilities

  • 🎙️ meetcap
    Records a meeting as two separate tracks — your microphone and everyone else's audio — and transcribes them locally with Whisper. Because the sides never mix during capture, the transcript knows who spoke without any diarization model, and one recorder dying does not take the other with it. Plays back either side or both mixed, highlights the transcript as the audio plays, and picks the largest Whisper model the machine can actually finish. Linux desktop app (PipeWire), not a browser applet; nothing leaves your computer.
  • 📜 Teleprompter
    A single-file browser teleprompter for recording short video lessons and podcasts. Load a script from a .txt file or paste it directly, adjust scroll speed and font size live, pick a reading theme, and save multiple scripts for reuse. Works offline, no account; great as an OBS Browser Source — drop the URL into OBS and it scrolls inside your recording scene.

Research

  • 🗣️ verbatim
    Records a student's conversation with an AI exactly as it happens, and hands back one JSON file per student ready for analysis. Every turn is tagged student or ai at the moment it is made, so who said what is a fact rather than something inferred from a transcript afterwards. The activity is yours — swap the prompt and the closing questions and the same instrument runs a different study — and a built-in calculator, grapher and table of values are recorded alongside the conversation.

About

These applets are designed for hands-on mathematical exploration in the classroom. Each applet is self-contained with its own README and can be used independently.
