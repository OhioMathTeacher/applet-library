Primes and subtraction · Grades 5–8 · Unsolved

Gilbreath's Conjecture

List the primes. Subtract neighbors to make a new row. Keep going. Every new row starts with 1.

Try it

2, 3, 5, 7, 11…1, 2, 2, 4…1, 0, 2…

What we know

Norman Gilbreath noticed it in 1958, doodling on a napkin.

Computers have checked hundreds of billions of rows. It has never failed.

Ask your students

Why do the rows fill up with 0s and 2s?

Start with a different list, like 1, 2, 4, 8, 16. Does it still work?
