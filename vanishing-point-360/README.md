# Vanishing Point 360

Vanishing Point on a vector monitor, with three ways to play. A retro shooter for an NES-style controller.

## How to play

Choose a game on the title screen with Up and Down, then press Start.

- **Classic.** Five beams run off to a single vanishing point on the horizon. Hop between them, shoot fifteen white saucers to clear each sector, then try to torpedo the sector ship as it crosses the far end of the field.
- **360.** The beams wrap into a tube, and you ride its rim as a claw. Every sector changes the tube's shape (octagon, square, star, U, steps, diamond) and its colors. The sector ship circles the far end once.
- **Curl.** Flat sectors, and every third one the beams curl up into a tube, then lay flat again.

| Button | Keyboard | Does |
|---|---|---|
| D-pad | Arrows | Hop from beam to beam. Up and Down choose a game on the title screen. |
| A | Z or K | Laser. |
| B | X or J | Torpedo: three per sector. Torpedoes destroy anything; lasers bounce off some things. |
| Select | Shift | Side view: the same scene seen from the side, showing how the picture is drawn in perspective. Inside the tube it shows a slice down the tube's length. |
| Start | Enter | Play / pause. |

M mutes the sound. A standard USB gamepad works too.

Later sectors bring new things down the beams: yellow chirpers are a bonus, sparks are to dodge, and green blockers can only be torpedoed or avoided.

The screen is drawn the way a 1981 vector monitor drew it: lines of light on phosphor that glow and fade, brighter at the corners where the beam turned. The **PHOSPHOR** button in the bottom corner sets how long the glow lasts, how much it blooms, and whether the beam flickers. V and G step through the same settings from the keyboard, and F toggles fullscreen.

High scores are kept for each game separately, in this browser on this computer.
