The openers

Reach
A math game a class plays together: a few digits, a target, and the question is not "can you reach it" but "how many ways are there, and how do we know we have them all?" Free, no accounts, no ads. Teachers start a room at reach.mathaiproject.org/#teacher; students join with a code.
Name Game
Learn your students' names from their photos. Study walks the roster with names shown; Quiz hides them. Downloads for Windows, Mac and Linux are on the releases page; nothing is uploaded anywhere, and the photos stay on your computer. MIT licensed.

The three

One move, three difficulties: ask the text. All three run in a browser, use whatever AI model you point them at — a free one on your own machine, or a cloud one with your own key — and none of them send your reading, questions or notes anywhere.

Allegory · lyrics
A music player for a folder of your own music, with Socrates in the sidebar: highlight a line, ask a question. Not yet published; it runs on your own computer against your own music, and will be here when it is ready.
Clique · words
A Connections-style puzzle maker for the classroom, in English, Spanish and Chinese, with Ask Socrates while you play. Build your own, share it as a link. Code.
Marginalia · text
A close reader for the hardest texts — Plato's cave among them: highlight, ask, write in the margin. Your notes stay yours. Code.

Clique and Marginalia open in your browser from those links, nothing to install. All three are free software under the AGPL: copy them, change them, give them away.

One thing to know if you run a model locally: a page served over https — like the two links above — is not allowed by the browser to talk to a model on localhost. A cloud key works from there; for a local model, download the app and open it from your own computer, or use the copy on the thumbdrive.

Running a free model yourself

What ran during the talk was a 3-billion-parameter model, on an ordinary laptop, with the wifi off. Here is the whole recipe.

  1. Install Ollama (Mac, Windows or Linux — one download).
  2. In a terminal, fetch the model. About two gigabytes, once: 
    ollama pull llama3.2:3b
  3. Open any of the three apps and choose Local in its AI Setup. It finds the model by itself.

Any laptop from the last few years with 8 GB of memory will do. It is faster with a graphics card and fine without one. llama3.2:3b is Meta's, released under the Llama 3.2 Community License; Ollama's library has plenty of others to try.
